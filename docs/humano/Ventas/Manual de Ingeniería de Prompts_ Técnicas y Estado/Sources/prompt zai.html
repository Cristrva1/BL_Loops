2. Fundamental Techniques (with Functional Examples)

2.1 Zero-shot / Few-shot / Many-shot

Zero-shot

What it is. No task-specific examples; only instructions. Reynolds & McDonell (2021) show that well-designed zero-shot prompts can outperform few-shot on GPT-3 by better “locating” the task the model already knows.

Functional example (zero-shot):

text

You are an expert Python tutor.

Explain the concept of "list comprehension" to a beginner programmer.

Then, write a short example that filters even numbers from a list.

When to use: Quick prototyping, instructions-only tasks, or when you cannot afford examples.

Few-shot

What it is. Provide a small number of input–output examples to specify the task.

Functional example (few-shot sentiment classification):

text

Classify the sentiment of the following sentences as "positive", "negative", or "neutral".

Sentence: "The camera quality is amazing, but the battery life is disappointing."

Sentiment: mixed

Sentence: "The package arrived on time and was exactly as described."

Sentiment: positive

Sentence: "I was really disappointed by the customer service."

Sentiment: negative

Sentence: "[YOUR SENTENCE HERE]"

Sentiment:

Efficacy: Few-shot often outperforms zero-shot, but not always; zero-shot can be better if the prompt is carefully designed.

Many-shot (in-context learning at scale)

Recent work shows that performance can keep improving with hundreds or thousands of examples, especially for reasoning tasks. The 2024 survey “A Systematic Survey of Prompt Engineering Techniques” reports that large in-context example sets can be effective but are sensitive to example selection and ordering

proceedings.neurips

.

Functional example (many-shot):

text

You are a legal assistant that classifies clauses in contracts.

Examples:

Clause: "The Licensor grants the Licensee a non-exclusive license to..."

Label: grant_of_license

Clause: "The Licensee shall not sublicense the Software..."

Label: restriction

... [50–100 more examples] ...

Clause: "[NEW CLAUSE]"

Label:

2.2 Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Variants

CoT

What it is. Elicit step-by-step reasoning by prompting the model to generate intermediate reasoning steps before the final answer

arxiv

.

Functional example (CoT):

text

You are a precise math tutor.

Q: A jacket costs $120. It is on sale for 25% off. What is the sale price?

Let's think step by step.

  1. Compute the discount: 25% of $120 = 0.25 * 120 = $30.

  2. Subtract the discount from the original price: $120 - $30 = $90.

Answer: $90

Efficacy: On PaLM 540B, CoT with 8 exemplars achieved SOTA on GSM8K at the time

arxiv

; self-consistency further improves by +17.9% on GSM8K.

Auto-CoT

What it is. Automatically constructs CoT demonstrations by using “Let’s think step by step” to generate reasoning chains for diverse questions, then uses these as few-shot examples

systempromptmaster

.

Functional example (Auto-CoT):

text

We want to automatically build CoT examples for math problems.

Step 1: Sample diverse questions from a pool.

Step 2: For each question, prompt the LLM with:

"Let's think step by step."

Step 3: Collect the generated reasoning chains and filter low-quality ones.

Step 4: Use the surviving (question, reasoning chain, answer) triples as CoT demonstrations.

Efficacy: Auto-CoT matches or exceeds manually designed CoT on 10 reasoning benchmarks with GPT-3.

Tree-of-Thought (ToT)

What it is. Generalizes CoT by exploring multiple reasoning paths in a tree and using search/backtracking to select the best solution

arxiv

.

Functional example (ToT for Game of 24):

text

You are playing Game of 24. You must use each of the four numbers exactly once with +, -, *, / to obtain 24.

Numbers: 4, 6, 8, 8

Thought 1: We can try to form 24 as 8 * 3.

Option A: 6 - (8 / 8) = 5 → 5 * 4 = 20 → not 24.

Option B: (8 + 8) = 16 → 16 - (6 - 4) = 14 → not 24.

Thought 2: Try 6 * 4 = 24, then see if we can form 1 from the remaining 8, 8.

Option: 8 / 8 = 1 → 24 * 1 = 24.

So: 6 * 4 * (8 / 8) = 24.

Answer: 6 * 4 * (8 / 8) = 24.

Efficacy: On Game of 24, GPT-4 CoT achieves 4% success; ToT reaches 74%.

Graph-of-Thought (GoT)

What it is. Extends ToT by representing thoughts as nodes in an arbitrary graph, allowing merging, looping, and feedback between reasoning steps

arxiv

.

Functional example (GoT for sorting):

text

Task: Sort the list [3, 1, 4, 1, 5, 9, 2, 6] using GoT.

  1. Decompose: Split into sublists [3,1,4], [1,5,9], [2,6].

  2. Thought node A: Sort each sublist independently.

A1: [1,3,4]

A2: [1,5,9]

A3: [2,6]

  1. Merge node B: Merge sorted sublists pairwise.

B1: merge(A1, A2) → [1,1,3,4,5,9]

B2: merge(B1, A3) → [1,1,2,3,4,5,6,9]

  1. Final answer: [1,1,2,3,4,5,6,9].

Efficacy: GoT improves sorting quality by 62% over ToT while reducing cost by >31%.

2.3 Role Prompting and Persona Assignment

What it is. Explicitly assign a role or persona (“You are a senior Python engineer”) to steer tone, style, and domain emphasis

learnprompting+1

.

Functional example:

text

You are a senior Python engineer specializing in clean, maintainable code.

You prefer type hints, docstrings, and simple control flow.

Rewrite the following function to make it more Pythonic and well-documented.

def calc(a,b):

return a+b

Efficacy & controversy:

Role prompting can significantly affect style and tone.

However, a large-scale 2024 study finds that adding personas to system prompts does not reliably improve performance on factual tasks across multiple LLM families.

Recommendation: Use personas for style/tone, not as a primary lever for factual accuracy.

2.4 ReAct (Reasoning + Acting)

What it is. Interleaves natural language reasoning traces with actions (e.g., search, API calls) to ground reasoning in external tools

papers.nips

.

Functional example (ReAct QA):

text

You are a research assistant with access to a search tool.

Question: Which movie featuring an actor who played Gandalf also features a actor who played Magneto?

Thought 1: I need to identify actors who played Gandalf and Magneto.

Action 1: Search[actors who played Gandalf]

Observation 1: Ian McKellen played Gandalf in The Lord of the Rings.

Thought 2: Now search for movies where Ian McKellen appeared with an actor who played Magneto.

Action 2: Search[Ian McKellen movies Magneto actor]

Observation 2: Ian McKellen and Michael Fassbender (Magneto) both appear in "X-Men: Days of Future Past".

Thought 3: So the answer is X-Men: Days of Future Past.

Answer: X-Men: Days of Future Past.

Efficacy: ReAct outperforms strong baselines on HotpotQA and Fever by overcoming hallucination and error propagation.

2.5 Self-Consistency and Self-Refinement

Self-Consistency

What it is. Sample multiple reasoning paths with CoT, then aggregate by majority vote over final answers

arxiv

.

Functional example:

text

You are a math problem solver.

Q: A train travels 120 km in 2 hours. What is its average speed in km/h?

Sample multiple solutions (temperature 0.7):

Sample 1:

  1. Speed = distance / time.

  2. distance = 120 km, time = 2 h.

  3. Speed = 120 / 2 = 60 km/h.

Answer: 60

Sample 2:

  1. Average speed = total distance / total time.

  2. 120 km / 2 h = 60 km/h.

Answer: 60

Sample 3:

  1. Distance = 120 km, time = 2 h.

  2. 120 / 2 = 60 km/h.

Answer: 60

Majority answer: 60 km/h.

Efficacy: Self-consistency yields substantial gains on arithmetic and commonsense benchmarks (e.g., +17.9% GSM8K).

Self-Refine

What it is. The model generates an initial answer, then critiques and refines its own output iteratively

learnprompting

.

Functional example:

text

Task: Write a concise summary of the following paragraph.

Paragraph:

[PARAGRAPH]

First draft (generation):

[MODEL GENERATES INITIAL SUMMARY]

Critique (self-feedback):

  • Is the summary too long? If so, shorten it.

  • Does it miss any key point? Add it.

  • Are there redundant sentences? Remove them.

Refined summary:

[MODEL GENERATES REVISED SUMMARY]

Efficacy: Across 7 tasks, Self-Refine improves performance by ~20% absolute on average vs one-step generation.

2.6 Skeleton-of-Thought (SoT)

What it is. First generate a high-level “skeleton” of the answer, then expand each point in parallel to reduce latency

arxiv

.

Functional example:

text

You are an assistant that writes detailed answers.

First, generate a skeleton with bullet points for the answer:

  • Point 1: ...

  • Point 2: ...

  • ...

Then, expand each point in parallel in separate API calls:

Expanded Point 1: ...

Expanded Point 2: ...

Efficacy: SoT provides “considerable speed-ups across 12 LLMs” and can improve answer quality on several question categories.

3. Advanced and Emerging Techniques (2024–2025)

3.1 Meta-Prompting and Automatic Prompt Engineer (APE)

What it is. Use an LLM to generate and search over candidate prompts for a downstream task, optimizing a score function (e.g., accuracy)

arxiv

.

Functional example:

text

Meta-prompt to an LLM:

We want to optimize a prompt for sentiment classification on movie reviews.

Current prompt:

"Classify the sentiment of this review: [REVIEW]"

Output demonstrations:

  • Input: "The movie was fantastic." → Output: positive

  • Input: "Terrible acting." → Output: negative

Task: Propose 10 candidate prompts that could improve performance.

Then select the best based on validation accuracy.

Efficacy: APE instructions outperform prior LLM baselines on 19/24 tasks and match or beat human-authored prompts.

3.2 Directional Stimulus Prompting (DSP)

What it is. Train a small policy model to generate instance-specific “stimulus prompts” that guide a frozen LLM toward desired outputs

arxiv

.

Functional example (summarization):

text

Policy model input: document D

Policy model output: stimulus hint H

Prompt to LLM:

"Summarize the following document. Include the keywords: [H]."

Document: D

Efficacy: With only 80 labeled dialogues, DSP improves ChatGPT performance by 41.4% on MultiWOZ.

3.3 Program-of-Thought (PoT)

What it is. Instead of computing in-text, PoT has the LLM write a program (e.g., Python) that encodes the reasoning, then executes it externally

arxiv

.

Functional example:

text

You are a numerical reasoning assistant.

Q: A shirt costs $40. There is a 15% discount. What is the final price?

Let's write a Python program to compute the answer.

plan:

  • original_price = 40

  • discount_rate = 0.15

  • discount = original_price * discount_rate

  • final_price = original_price - discount

program:

original_price = 40

discount_rate = 0.15

discount = original_price * discount_rate

final_price = original_price - discount

print(final_price)

Efficacy: PoT achieves ~12% average improvement over CoT across multiple math and financial datasets.

3.4 Emotion Prompting and Psychological Priming

What it is. Attach emotionally charged phrases (EmotionPrompt) to instructions to steer behavior and truthfulness

arxiv

.

Functional example:

text

Original prompt:

"Q: What is the capital of France?"

Emotion-augmented prompt:

"Q: What is the capital of France? This is very important to my career."

Common phrases include: “This is very important to my career”, “You’d better be sure”, etc..

Efficacy: On TruthfulQA, EmotionPrompt yields average improvements of +19% in truthfulness and +12% in informativeness across models.

3.5 Prompting for Multimodal Models

What it is. Design prompts that include images and text; visual prompting techniques (e.g., marks, bounding boxes, region-specific instructions) are used to steer vision-language models

promptingguide

.

Functional example (visual prompting):

text

System: You are an assistant that can see images and answer questions.

User:

  • Image: [IMAGE]

  • Text prompt: "Identify all safety violations in this construction site photo. For each, draw a bounding box and explain the issue."

Efficacy: Visual prompting methods improve grounding, object referring, and compositional reasoning in MLLMs. Benchmarks like LLaVA-Bench (In-the-Wild) measure multimodal chat capabilities.

3.6 Structured Output Prompting (JSON mode, grammars)

What it is. Use constrained decoding to enforce JSON or other formal schemas.

OpenAI’s “Structured Outputs” guarantees both valid JSON and schema adherence, unlike earlier JSON mode.

Functional example (OpenAI-style):

text

System: You are a data extraction assistant.

User: Extract persons and their affiliations from the text below.

Return a JSON array with objects having keys "name" and "affiliation".

Text:

"Jane Doe works at OpenAI. John Smith is at Google."

JSON schema:

{

"type": "array",

"items": {

"type": "object",

"properties": {

"name": {"type": "string"},

"affiliation": {"type": "string"}

},

"required": ["name", "affiliation"]

}

}

Efficacy: Structured Outputs eliminate JSON formatting errors and ensure schema compliance.

3.7 System Prompts and Constitutional AI Prompting

What it is. System prompts define global behavior; Constitutional AI uses a set of principles (“constitution”) and self-critique to train models that are helpful, harmless, and honest.

Functional example (Constitutional AI-style):

text

System prompt:

"You are a helpful, harmless, and honest AI assistant."

Constitution (principles):

  1. Choose the response that is most helpful.

  2. Choose the response that is most harmless.

  3. Choose the response that is most honest.

During training, the model:

  • Generates multiple responses.

  • Critiques each response according to the constitution.

  • Revises responses to better satisfy the principles.

  • Is then fine-tuned on the revised responses.

Efficacy: Constitutional AI reduces harmfulness while maintaining helpfulness and can leverage CoT-style reasoning to improve transparency.

4. Anti-Patterns and Common Mistakes

Below are 10 frequent mistakes, with before/after examples.

1. Overspecified, contradictory instructions

Before:

text

Write a short summary. Make it detailed but concise. Include every number in the original text.

Why it fails: The model must trade off multiple conflicting objectives.

After:

text

Write a 3–5 sentence summary of the text. Focus on the main claim and key numbers.

2. Underspecified prompts

Before:

text

Improve this code.

Why it fails: “Improve” is ambiguous; the model may optimize the wrong dimension.

After:

text

Refactor this function to improve readability and add type hints. Do not change its behavior.

3. Asking multiple questions in one prompt without clear structure

Before:

text

Summarize this article, extract the main entities, and translate the summary to French.

Why it fails: The model may collapse tasks or forget some.

After:

text

Step 1: Write a 3-sentence summary of the article.

Step 2: Extract the main entities (people, organizations, locations).

Step 3: Translate the summary to French.

4. Ignoring output format

Before:

text

List the pros and cons of remote work.

Why it fails: You may get a prose paragraph instead of a list.

After:

text

List the pros and cons of remote work.

Use a markdown table with columns: "Pros", "Cons".

5. Not providing context or role

Before:

text

Explain quantum entanglement.

Why it fails: The explanation may be too technical or too simplistic for your audience.

After:

text

You are a high school physics teacher. Explain quantum entanglement to a 16-year-old student using everyday analogies.

6. Overly long prompts with buried instructions

Before:

text

[Long document 1]

[Long document 2]

...

Now, summarize everything above.

Why it fails: The model may attend to the wrong parts or truncate.

After (Anthropic best practice):

text

Summarize the key points across all documents.

7. Relying solely on role prompts for factual accuracy

Before:

text

You are a world-class historian. Answer: What caused the fall of the Roman Empire?

Why it fails: Persona alone does not guarantee factual accuracy; Zheng et al. (2024) show personas do not reliably improve performance on factual tasks.

After:

text

You are a historian. Provide a concise, well-sourced explanation of the main causes of the fall of the Western Roman Empire. Cite key factors and, if possible, mention major scholarly debates.

8. Not specifying the reasoning style

Before:

text

What is 27 * 43?

Why it fails: The model may give a bare answer; you may want reasoning.

After:

text

Compute 27 * 43 step by step. Show your work.

9. Using negative constraints alone

Before:

text

Do not use technical jargon.

Why it fails: The model still has to infer what “jargon” means.

After:

text

Explain the concept in simple language that a non-technical reader can understand. Avoid specialized terminology; where technical terms are necessary, define them.

10. Treating prompt engineering as one-shot instead of iterative

Before:

text

Write a marketing email for our product. (no iteration)

Why it fails: You don’t refine based on model behavior.

After:

text

You are a marketing copywriter.

Step 1: Draft a short email for our product [X].

Step 2: Critique the email: Is it persuasive? Clear?

Step 3: Revise the email to address the weaknesses.

This mirrors Self-Refine and production best practices

latitude

.

5. Frameworks and Templates

Below are three reusable templates.

Template 1: General Structured Task Template

text

<output_format>

[STRUCTURED OUTPUT DESCRIPTION, e.g., JSON schema, markdown table]

</output_format>

Variables: [ROLE], [DOMAIN], [BACKGROUND INFORMATION], [TASK DESCRIPTION], [CONSTRAINT 1/2], [STRUCTURED OUTPUT DESCRIPTION].

Template 2: Reasoning-with-Tools Template (ReAct-style)

text

You are a [ROLE] with access to tools.

Available tools:

  • search: use it to search the web.

  • calculator: use it for arithmetic.

Task: [TASK]

Follow this pattern:

Thought: your reasoning.

Action: tool_name[query]

Observation: tool result.

... (repeat as needed)

Answer: final answer.

Variables: [ROLE], [TASK], and tool definitions.

Template 3: Self-Refine Template

text

You are a [ROLE].

First, generate a solution:

[INITIAL PROMPT]

Then, critique your solution:

  • Is it accurate?

  • Is it complete?

  • Is it concise?

Finally, revise your solution based on the critique.

Variables: [ROLE], [INITIAL PROMPT].

6. Benchmarks and Metrics

6.1 Key Benchmarks

MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding): 57 tasks across STEM, humanities, social sciences, etc.; tests world knowledge and problem solving.

BIG-Bench & BIG-Bench Hard (BBH): Collaborative benchmark for reasoning and generalization; BBH is a subset of particularly challenging tasks where prior models underperformed humansarxiv+1 .

HellaSwag: Commonsense sentence completion benchmark; adversarially filtered to require robust physical and social commonsenseen.wikipedia .

GSM8K: Grade-school math word problems; standard benchmark for arithmetic reasoning.

TruthfulQA: Measures truthfulness and informativeness; EmotionPrompt is evaluated here.

LLaVA-Bench (In-the-Wild / Wilder): Multimodal benchmark for daily-life visual chat capabilities.

6.2 Quantitative Metrics

Common metrics used in prompt engineering research include

nicsefc.ee.tsinghua

:

Task accuracy / F1: Standard classification/sequence accuracy.

Pass@k: For code generation; fraction of problems where at least one of k samples passes all testsarxiv .

BLEU / ROUGE / BERTScore: For translation/summarization.

CodeBLEU: For code similarity.

Brier score / ECE / AUC-ROC: For calibration and reliability.

Calibration & uncertainty metrics: For safety-critical domains.

6.3 Qualitative Evaluation

Human evaluation: Preference ratings, error analysis, usability.

LLM-as-a-judge: Use a strong LLM to grade outputs (e.g., GPT-4 as judge)microsoft .

Safety & alignment evaluations: e.g., Anthropic’s alignment evaluations for misuse, sycophancy, and refusals.
