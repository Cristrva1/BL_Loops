2. FUNDAMENTAL TECHNIQUES — WITH FUNCTIONAL EXAMPLES

2.1 Zero-Shot / Few-Shot / Many-Shot

Zero-Shot: The model receives only the task description, with no examples.

[ZERO-SHOT EXAMPLE]

System: You are an expert medical coder.

User: Classify the following clinical note as "Inpatient" or "Outpatient":

"Patient presented to the ER with chest pain. Discharged after 4 hours of observation."

→ Expected: Outpatient

Few-Shot: Two to eight input-output demonstrations precede the query. Brown et al. (2020) showed that 4–8 shots often close the gap with fine-tuned models on classification tasks.

[FEW-SHOT EXAMPLE — Sentiment]

User:

Review: "The battery drains in 2 hours." → Negative

Review: "Arrived a day early, perfect packaging." → Positive

Review: "Works as advertised, nothing special." → Neutral

Review: "Screen flickers constantly, very annoying." → ?

→ Expected: Negative

Many-Shot (2024): Agarwal et al. (2024, arXiv 2404.11018) demonstrated that scaling to hundreds or thousands of examples in a long-context model (e.g., Gemini 1.5 Pro, 1M token window) yields strong gains on tasks where few-shot saturates. Empirical finding: diminishing returns typically appear after ~100–200 shots; diversity of examples matters more than raw count.

2.2 Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Variants

Standard CoT (Wei et al. 2022): Include one or more reasoning demonstrations.

[CoT EXAMPLE — Arithmetic]

Q: A store has 48 apples. It sells 3/4 of them in the morning and

receives a new shipment of 20. How many apples are there now?

A: Let me think step by step.

Step 1: Sold = 48 × 0.75 = 36 apples.

Step 2: Remaining = 48 − 36 = 12 apples.

Step 3: After shipment = 12 + 20 = 32 apples.

Answer: 32

Zero-Shot CoT (Kojima et al. 2022): Simply appending "Let's think step by step" activates chain-of-thought reasoning without examples. Outperforms standard zero-shot by ~20% on GSM8K.

Auto-CoT (Zhang et al. 2022, arXiv 2210.11610): Automatically clusters questions and generates CoT demonstrations via another model call, removing the need for human annotation while maintaining performance comparable to manual CoT.

Tree-of-Thought (ToT — Yao et al. 2023): Maintains a tree of intermediate reasoning states, evaluated via BFS/DFS. On the Game of 24 task: IO prompting = 7.3%, CoT = 4%, CoT-SC = 9%, ToT (b=5) = 74% (arXiv 2305.10601 / NeurIPS 2023).

[ToT CONCEPTUAL STRUCTURE — Game of 24 with inputs: 4, 9, 10, 13]

Root: [4, 9, 10, 13]

├─ Thought A: 13−9=4 → [4, 4, 10] → "maybe"

│ ├─ Thought A1: 10−4=6 → [4, 6] → "sure"

│ │ └─ 4×6=24 ✓ ← SOLUTION

│ └─ Thought A2: 4×4=16 → [10, 16] → "impossible"

├─ Thought B: 9+4=13 → [13, 10, 13] → "maybe"

└─ ... (BFS explores breadth b=5, prunes "impossible" branches)

Graph-of-Thought (GoT — Yao et al. 2023b, arXiv 2308.09687): Extends ToT to arbitrary DAG structures, allowing reasoning paths to merge. Particularly effective for problems requiring aggregation of multiple evidence threads.

⚠️ Critical Contradiction (2024): Stechly et al. (NeurIPS 2024, Proceedings) found that CoT performance degrades sharply when problems generalize beyond the narrow distribution of examples. On synthetic planning problems, accuracy dropped nearly to zero with slight complexity increases. This challenges the claim that CoT "unlocks general reasoning." Both Wei et al.'s positive findings and Stechly et al.'s limitations should be held simultaneously.

2.3 Role Prompting and Persona Assignment

Assigning the model a specific role ("You are a senior security researcher with 20 years of experience...") consistently improves domain-aligned outputs. The effect is strongest in domains with well-defined expert language.

[ROLE PROMPTING EXAMPLE]

System: You are a board-certified cardiologist reviewing patient ECG reports.

Your responses must:

  • Cite specific ECG criteria (e.g., QRS duration, PR interval)

  • Flag any life-threatening findings immediately

  • End with a priority level: [STAT / Urgent / Routine]

User: 60-year-old male. ECG shows: Wide QRS complex (140ms),

Left bundle branch block morphology, Heart rate 35 bpm.

Schulhoff et al. (2024) list role prompting as one of the six major technique categories in their 58-technique taxonomy.

2.4 ReAct (Reasoning + Acting)

ReAct (Yao et al. 2022, arXiv 2210.03629 / ICLR 2023) interleaves Thought → Action → Observation cycles, grounding reasoning in external tool calls.

[ReAct EXAMPLE — Knowledge-intensive QA]

Question: What is the elevation range for the area the eastern sector

of the Colorado orogeny extends into?

Thought 1: I need to find where the eastern sector of Colorado orogeny extends.

Action 1: Search[Colorado orogeny]

Obs 1: "The Colorado orogeny was an episode of mountain building...

The eastern sector extends into the High Plains."

Thought 2: Now I need the elevation of the High Plains.

Action 2: Search[High Plains elevation]

Obs 2: "The High Plains rise from around 1,800 feet to 7,000 feet above sea level."

Thought 3: I have enough information.

Action 3: Finish[1,800 to 7,000 feet]

Benchmark results (Yao et al. 2022): ReAct outperforms pure Act on ALFWorld (+34% absolute) and WebShop. On HotPotQA (EM): CoT=29.4%, ReAct=27.4% (slight disadvantage on pure QA), but ReAct+CoT combined = 35.1%. Note: A 2024 paper (Brittle Foundations of ReAct, arXiv 2405.13966) found that 40% of ReAct "thoughts" led to invalid actions in GPT-3.5-Instruct, rising to 80–90% in weaker models — signaling that reasoning quality is prerequisite for ReAct to function.

2.5 Self-Consistency and Self-Refinement

Self-Consistency (Wang et al. 2022, arXiv 2203.11171): Sample N diverse reasoning paths (temperature > 0), take majority vote. +10–15% over greedy CoT on GSM8K with N=40; scales with model size.

[SELF-CONSISTENCY PSEUDOCODE]

for i in range(N=20):

path_i = LLM(prompt, temperature=0.7)

answer_i = extract_answer(path_i)

final_answer = majority_vote(answer_i for all i)

Self-Refine (Madaan et al. 2023/NeurIPS 2024): The model iteratively generates output, critiques it, and revises.

[SELF-REFINE LOOP]

  1. Generate: [initial code solution]

  2. Critique: "This code has O(n²) complexity; there's a more efficient approach."

  3. Refine: [improved O(n log n) solution]

→ Repeat until quality threshold met or max iterations reached

Madaan et al. report +20% on code generation; the technique was adopted into OpenAI o1-style "extended thinking."

2.6 Skeleton-of-Thought

Proposed by Ning et al. (2023, arXiv 2307.15337), this approach first generates a high-level outline (skeleton), then fills in each section in parallel , dramatically increasing throughput.

[SKELETON-OF-THOUGHT EXAMPLE]

Phase 1 → Generate skeleton:

"Write an essay on climate change."

Skeleton:

[1. Introduction] [2. Causes] [3. Effects] [4. Solutions] [5. Conclusion]

Phase 2 → Parallel expansion:

Thread A: Expand [Introduction] → ...

Thread B: Expand [Causes] → ...

Thread C: Expand [Effects] → ...

(All executed simultaneously, then concatenated)

Reported speedup: 2.39× with comparable quality on generation tasks. Best suited for models/systems that support parallel decoding.

3. ADVANCED AND EMERGING TECHNIQUES (2024–2025)

3.1 Meta-Prompting and Automatic Prompt Engineer (APE)

APE (Zhou et al. 2022, arXiv 2211.01910): Treats prompt generation as black-box optimization. An "inference LLM" proposes candidate instruction strings from I/O demonstrations; a "scoring LLM" evaluates them via execution on a validation set.

Key finding: APE discovered the instruction "Let's work this out in a step by step way to be sure we have the right answer" — which outperformed the manually-designed "Let's think step by step" on MultiArith.

OPRO (Yang et al. 2024, arXiv 2309.03409 — Google DeepMind): LLMs as Optimizers. Phrases like "Take a deep breath" were discovered by the optimizer and shown to improve math problem-solving performance. OPRO iteratively refines prompt instructions by including past performance scores in the meta-prompt.

DSPy (Khattab et al. 2024, ICLR 2024): The most practically influential automated optimization framework as of 2025. Treats prompt engineering as a declarative compilation problem . Key components:

Signatures : Define input/output behavior declaratively

Modules (e.g., dspy.ChainOfThought, dspy.ReAct)

Teleprompters/Optimizers : BootstrapFewShot, MIPRO, etc.

Results: DSPy-optimized prompt vs. 20 hours of human engineering → AI-generated prompt achieved F1 ≈ 0.6 vs. inferior human prompt (Schulhoff 2024, learnprompting.org). In DSPy studies on LegalBench and customer service tasks (2024-2025), accuracy gains of 10–20% over manual prompts were reported.

python

DSPy EXAMPLE — Declarative RAG

import dspy

class GenerateAnswer(dspy.Signature):

"""Answer questions with short factoid answers."""

context = dspy.InputField(desc="retrieved passages")

question = dspy.InputField()

answer = dspy.OutputField(desc="concise, factual answer")

class RAG(dspy.Module):

def init(self):

self.generate_answer = dspy.ChainOfThought(GenerateAnswer)

def forward(self, question):

context = retrieve(question)

return self.generate_answer(context=context, question=question)

Optimize: DSPy automatically selects best few-shot examples

optimizer = dspy.BootstrapFewShot(metric=accuracy_metric)

compiled_rag = optimizer.compile(RAG(), trainset=trainset)

### 3.2 Directional Stimulus Prompting (DSP)

Li et al. (2023, arXiv 2302.11520) introduce a small, fine-tuned auxiliary "hint generator" that produces stimulus/keyword hints prepended to the main prompt. The hints guide the black-box LLM toward specific, desired outputs without modifying the frozen LLM.

[DSP EXAMPLE — Summarization]

Stimulus (from hint generator): [FOCUS: economic impact, supply chain disruption]

Prompt: Using the above focus keywords as directional guidance,

summarize the following news article in 3 sentences:

[ARTICLE TEXT]

Reported: ~14% improvement on summarization benchmarks vs. standard prompting (Li et al. 2023).

3.3 Program-of-Thought (PoT)

Chen et al. (2022/TMLR 2023) separate reasoning from computation : the LLM generates a Python/code program expressing the reasoning, and an external interpreter executes it.

python

PoT EXAMPLE — Financial calculation

"""

Problem: A bond has a face value of $1000, coupon rate 5%, YTM 4%, 5 years to maturity.

What is its price?

Program:

"""

face_value = 1000

coupon_rate = 0.05

ytm = 0.04

years = 5

coupon = face_value * coupon_rate

pv_coupons = sum([coupon / (1 + ytm)**t for t in range(1, years+1)])

pv_face = face_value / (1 + ytm)**years

price = pv_coupons + pv_face

print(f"Bond price: ${price:.2f}") # → $1044.52


PoT consistently outperforms CoT on computation-heavy tasks (MATH, FinQA, TabMWP) because it offloads exact arithmetic to a deterministic interpreter, eliminating calculation errors.

### 3.4 Emotion Prompting and Psychological Priming

**EmotionPrompt (Li et al. 2023/2024, arXiv 2307.11760):** Appending psychologically-grounded emotional stimuli to prompts measurably improves LLM performance. Based on three psychological frameworks: Self-Monitoring, Social Cognitive Theory, and Cognitive Emotion Regulation.

[EMOTIONPROMPT EXAMPLE]

Standard: "Explain the implications of quantum entanglement for cryptography."

EmotionPrompt EP01: "Explain the implications of quantum entanglement for cryptography.

This is very important to my career."

EmotionPrompt EP07: "Explain the implications of quantum entanglement for cryptography.

Are you sure that's your final answer? Believe in your abilities and strive for excellence.

Your hard work will yield remarkable results."


**Quantitative results:**

- +8.00% relative improvement on Instruction Induction benchmark

- +115% on BIG-Bench (highly variable; task-dependent)

- Human study (n=106): +10.9% average on performance, truthfulness, and responsibility dimensions

**⚠️ Important caveat:** Stechly et al. (2024) and multiple 2024 replication studies suggest emotional stimuli effects may be model-specific and task-sensitive. Results are inconsistent across model families.

**NegativePrompt (IJCAI 2024):** Negative emotional stimuli (stress, social comparison) showed *more consistent* improvements than positive ones across 45 tasks — counter-intuitively, "competitive pressure" framing may more reliably focus model attention.

### 3.5 Prompting for Multimodal Models (Vision + Text)

Multimodal CoT was extended beyond language-only models in 2024. The framework:

[MULTIMODAL CoT — Two-Stage Structure]

Stage 1 (Rationale Generation):

Input: [IMAGE of a chart] + "What trend does this show?"

→ Generate: "The chart shows exponential growth from 2019-2023,

with a plateau in Q4 2023..."

Stage 2 (Answer Inference):

Input: [IMAGE] + Rationale from Stage 1 + "What year had the steepest growth?"

→ Final Answer: "2021"


For GPT-4V, Gemini 1.5, and Claude 3, visual inputs in EmotionPrompt experiments showed **15.96% improvement** vs. verbal-only 12.82% — suggesting visual priming is more potent than textual priming (Li et al. 2024 multimodal extension).

Best practices (Anthropic Claude 4 docs, 2025):

- Place image context *before* the question

- Use `<image_description>` XML tags to supplement visual context for Claude

- For multi-image tasks, explicitly number and label each image

## 4. ANTI-PATTERNS AND COMMON MISTAKES

*Based on Schulhoff et al. 2024, Anthropic best practices documentation, Sahoo et al. 2024, and production engineering experience.*

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 1: Vague Task Specification

**Why it fails:** LLMs are next-token predictors; ambiguous prompts generate high-entropy, unhelpful responses.

| Before | After |

|--------|-------|

| "Write something about climate change." | "Write a 3-paragraph executive briefing on the economic risks of climate change for a Fortune 500 CFO. Use data from 2022–2024. Tone: authoritative and concise." |

---

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 2: Forbidding Behavior Without Specifying Alternatives (Negative-Only Prompting)

**Why it fails:** Anthropic's Zack Witten (AI Engineer 2024 conf.) noted that *"telling Claude too forcefully what not to do can backfire via reverse psychology effect"*. Negative constraints also consume tokens without building desired behavior.

| Before | After |

|--------|-------|

| "Don't give me a list. Don't use bullet points. Don't be too long." | "Respond in continuous prose, maximum 3 paragraphs, using an analytical tone." |

---

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 3: Placing Instructions After Long Context

**Why it fails:** In long prompts (>20K tokens), instructions at the end suffer from "lost in the middle" attention degradation (Liu et al. 2023, arXiv 2307.03172).

| Before | After |

|--------|-------|

| [50-page document] ... [instructions at end] | [Instructions at start] + [50-page document] + [brief query repetition at end] |

*Anthropic Claude 4 docs report up to 30% quality improvement from query placement at the end after long documents.*

---

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 4: Over-Reliance on CoT for Simple Tasks

**Why it fails:** Wei et al. (2022) themselves noted CoT provides little or negative gains for single-step problems. Stechly et al. (NeurIPS 2024) showed performance can *decrease* when CoT is applied to tasks where direct answer is faster/simpler.

| Before | After |

|--------|-------|

| "What is 15% of 200? Think step by step." | "What is 15% of 200?" (Direct; no CoT needed) |

Use CoT for **multi-step** reasoning; skip it for lookup, single-step arithmetic, or classification.

---

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 5: Using a Single Example as a Few-Shot Demonstration

**Why it fails:** One example introduces bias without providing the model enough signal for robust generalization. Research (Schulhoff et al. 2024) indicates 3–8 diverse examples are optimal for most tasks.

| Before | After |

|--------|-------|

| "Sentiment: 'Great product!' → Positive\nSentiment: 'Terrible experience.' → ?" | 3–5 examples covering positive, negative, neutral, and edge cases (e.g., sarcasm) |

---

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 6: Unstructured Multi-Part Instructions

**Why it fails:** LLMs process all tokens, but follow-through on multi-part instructions degrades with more components. Without structure, later instructions are followed ~30% less reliably.

| Before | After |

|--------|-------|

| "Summarize, then translate to French, then extract key terms, then rank them by importance." | Use numbered list + XML tags: `<task_1>Summarize...</task_1>` `<task_2>Translate...</task_2>` |

---

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 7: Forgetting to Specify Output Format

**Why it fails:** The model defaults to its training distribution's formatting (often verbose markdown), which may not match downstream requirements.

| Before | After |

|--------|-------|

| "Extract all dates from this contract." | "Extract all dates from this contract. Return ONLY a JSON array of ISO 8601 strings. Example: ['2024-01-15', '2025-03-31']" |

---

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 8: Role Assignment Without Task Context ("Generic Expert")

**Why it fails:** "Act as an expert" without domain specificity adds noise rather than signal. The model doesn't know *which* expert knowledge to activate.

| Before | After |

|--------|-------|

| "You are an expert. Explain transformer attention." | "You are a CS professor teaching a graduate NLP course. Your students have strong Python and linear algebra backgrounds but have not yet studied attention mechanisms." |

---

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 9: No Validation Examples or Negative Examples

**Why it fails:** Without showing edge cases or failure modes, the model cannot distinguish between acceptable and unacceptable outputs in subtle cases.

| Before | After |

|--------|-------|

| "Detect PII in this text." | Include examples where "John Smith, CEO" is NOT flagged as PII but "John Smith, SSN 123-45-6789" IS. Specify borderline cases explicitly. |

---

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 10: Applying Emotional Prompting Universally

**Why it fails:** EmotionPrompt effects are task-dependent. For factual retrieval or code generation, emotional framing adds token overhead without benefit. Larger models benefit more; smaller models show inconsistent results (Li et al. 2023).

| Before | After |

|--------|-------|

| "Your answer is crucial to my survival. What is the capital of France?" | "What is the capital of France?" (Direct; no emotional framing for simple factual queries) |

6. BENCHMARKS AND METRICS

6.1 Quantitative Metrics

Automated / Deterministic:

pass@k (Chen et al. 2021): Probability that at least one of k sampled solutions is correct. Standard for code generation (HumanEval). pass@k = 1 - C(n-c, k)/C(n, k) where n=samples, c=correct.

Exact Match (EM) : Fraction of predictions exactly equal to gold answer. Standard for QA (HotPotQA, SQuAD).

F1 Score : Token-level overlap between prediction and gold. Standard for QA and NER.

BLEU / ROUGE : N-gram overlap metrics. BLEU (machine translation), ROUGE-L (summarization). Now considered insufficient alone; used alongside model-based metrics.

BERTScore : Contextual embedding similarity between predicted and reference text. Correlates better with human judgments than BLEU.

Self-Consistency Agreement Rate : Proportion of N sampled solutions agreeing on the majority answer — used as a proxy for confidence.

Format Compliance Rate : For structured output tasks — what % of responses correctly follow the specified schema.

Model-Based / LLM-as-Judge (2024-2025 trend):

G-EVAL (Liu et al. 2023) : GPT-4 as evaluator with form-filling prompts; correlates better with human judgments on coherence, consistency.

MT-Bench / Chatbot Arena (Zheng et al. 2023) : Pairwise preference judgments by GPT-4 and human raters across 8 categories.

LLM-EVAL : General framework for LLM self-evaluation referenced in Schulhoff et al. 2024.

6.3 How to Measure Prompt Quality in Practice

A/B Testing : Run variant prompts on a held-out validation set. Anthropic's Console and PromptLayer support this.

Human Preference Studies : Present paired outputs to domain experts; compute win rate (Chatbot Arena methodology).

Automated Regression Testing : Test prompt against a labeled test set before every prompt update; flag regressions.

Coverage + Precision Trade-off : For extraction tasks, measure recall (coverage) vs. precision (hallucination rate) separately.

Consistency Score : Run the same prompt 10× at temperature 0.7; measure variance in outputs — low variance = more robust prompt.

7. CURATED RESOURCES

7.1 Five Essential Papers

1. The Prompt Report: A Systematic Survey of Prompting Techniques

Schulhoff, Ilie et al. (2024, updated Feb 2025). arXiv 2406.06608.

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06608

The most comprehensive survey to date: 58 LLM techniques, 40 multimodal techniques, vocabulary of 33 terms, meta-analysis of 1,565 papers. Produced with OpenAI, Microsoft, Stanford, and University of Maryland collaboration.

2. Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models

Wei, Wang, Schuurmans et al. (2022). NeurIPS 2022. arXiv 2201.11903.

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903

The seminal CoT paper; demonstrates that 8 CoT exemplars can bring PaLM-540B to SOTA on GSM8K (58%), surpassing fine-tuned GPT-3. Establishes that CoT is an emergent property of scale (>100B parameters).

3. Tree of Thoughts: Deliberate Problem Solving with Large Language Models

Yao, Yu, Zhao et al. (2023). NeurIPS 2023. arXiv 2305.10601.

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10601

Introduces search-based reasoning for LLMs. Game of 24: CoT=4% → ToT=74%. Establishes that deliberate planning via BFS/DFS over thought trees dramatically outperforms sequential CoT on tasks requiring lookahead.

4. Large Language Models Understand and Can Be Enhanced by Emotional Stimuli (EmotionPrompt)

Li, Wang, Zhang et al. (2023/2024). arXiv 2307.11760.

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11760

First systematic study of psychological priming in LLM prompting. 11 emotional stimuli tested across 6 models and 45 tasks; +10.9% average human-rated improvement. Opens interdisciplinary HCI×NLP research direction.

5. Chain of Thoughtlessness? An Analysis of CoT in Planning

Stechly, Marquez, Kambhampati (2024). NeurIPS 2024. PDF: proceedings.neurips.cc

URL: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/3365d974ce309623bd8151082d78206c-Paper-Conference.pdf

Critical counterpoint: systematically shows CoT performance degrades when problems generalize slightly beyond the distribution of examples. Challenges the "emergent reasoning" narrative and provides a crucial calibration on CoT's actual scope.
